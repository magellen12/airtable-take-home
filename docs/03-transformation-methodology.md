# The Transformation Methodology

The repeatable motion the function runs. Designed to produce sound decisions when run by a
mid-tenure CSM without me in the room — that's the only design constraint that matters.

```
  DIAGNOSE  →  FIND THE CONSTRAINT  →  RUN THE PLAY  →  PRODUCE EVIDENCE  →  CARRY IT INTO THE RENEWAL
   45 min       automatic, from          3–10 wks       dated artifact       the commercial event
   with the     the scores                                                   it was all for
   customer
```

**One engine, stated once so it can't be confused: the *stage describes* where an account is;
the *binding constraint prescribes* what to do next.** Stage is the label you give the customer;
the constraint is the instruction you give the CSM.

---

## 1 · The maturity ladder (stage = description)

Five stages. A customer's stage *describes* where they are; it does not tell you what to do next.
That's the constraint's job.

| Stage | Name | What it looks like | Book |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | **Unaware** | Core product healthy. AI is not on the agenda and nobody has asked. | Harbor Lane |
| **1** | **Sponsored** | An executive has bought a vision. Nothing has been delivered. | TrailLine |
| **2** | **Contained** | Real AI value exists in one pocket and does not spread. | Corvus, Floor & Board, Voltaic |
| **3** | **Governed** | Sponsor + CoE + a review path exist. Scale is a governance question, not a belief question. | Meridian |
| **4** | **Compounding** | Customer builds and governs independently; expansion is customer-initiated and attributable to us. | *none in the book* |

**Stage 4 requires governance, not just velocity.** Voltaic is the reason this is stated
explicitly: it has Stage 4 build velocity and Stage 2 structure. Scoring it a 4 because it looks
impressive is the exact mistake that loses it. *Stage progression QoQ* is the headline
maturity-progression metric the function reports on.

## 2 · The four dimensions

Scored 1–5 in a working session with the customer, not from a desk. Any 4 or 5 requires written
evidence — that requirement is doing more work than the scale itself, because it's what stops a
relationship-led CSM from scoring their friendliest account a 5 on Sponsorship. The labels track
the vocabulary the function is measured on: adoption, sponsorship, governance, value.

| | **Adoption depth** | **Sponsorship & multi-threading** | **Governance maturity** | **Value evidence** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | No AI adoption; unaware | Nobody owns it, or it lives with one person | Nothing, or ungoverned shadow building | Nothing stated |
| **2** | One or two people experimenting | A champion, but below the decision line | Informal; a few people decide ad hoc | Anecdotes only |
| **3** | A team using AI with help | Exec engaged, single-threaded | A body exists but doesn't clear work | Internal numbers, not in buyer language |
| **4** | Team ships AI independently | Exec owns an outcome, 2+ threads | Working review path, no published standard | Quantified, not yet customer-validated |
| **5** | AI in production across teams; self-sufficient | Named sponsor, ≥3 threads, public commitment | CoE + review path + published standards | Validated, dated, repeated by the economic buyer |

## 3 · The constraint rule — the core mechanic (constraint = prescription)

> **An account's next move is set by its lowest dimension, not its average.**

This is what makes the methodology teachable. A CSM doesn't need fifteen years of pattern
recognition to know what to do next; they need to score honestly and read the lowest number.

The proof it works is Meridian: highest average in the book (3.50), producing nothing, because
its **Value evidence is a 2** and a review board is the gate — Sponsorship at 5 and Adoption at 4
don't open it. And Corvus: a below-average 2.25, one artifact away from a seven-figure expansion.

**Two tiebreak rules:**

1. **Ladder order** — when dimensions tie at the bottom, resolve in the order
   `Sponsorship → Adoption → Value evidence → Governance`. You need someone who owns it, then
   people adopting it, then evidence it worked, then structure to scale it.
2. **The Stage 0 exception** — *below Stage 1, Value evidence leads; at Stage 1 and above,
   Sponsorship leads.*

Rule 2 exists because the model gave a wrong answer the first time I ran it against Harbor Lane.
It wanted an executive sponsor recruited for something nobody at Harbor Lane has seen. **You
cannot sponsor an abstraction.** At Stage 0 you prove one narrow thing, and then you have
something a sponsor can attach themselves to. I'm keeping the failed version of the rule in the
doc on purpose — the model earned the second rule by being wrong once, and it's the kind of thing
the team should expect to keep doing to it.

**A note on "value evidence" as a constraint.** It doesn't always mean a dollar figure. The
evidence an account lacks is audience-specific: for a commercial buyer it's a cost-or-cycle-time
number (Play P4); for a governance review board it's a risk-and-controls case (Play P5). Same
constraint, different artifact. Meridian is the case — its binding constraint is value evidence,
but the evidence it needs is a security case, not a P&L line.

## 4 · The play library

Eight plays. Each is keyed to a **constraint**, not to an account, and each has an owner, a
partner, an artifact, and a definition of done. Deliberately small — a library of thirty plays is
a library nobody uses.

**The full library is [`05-play-library.md`](05-play-library.md)** — every play written in two
parts, *the standard* (global consistency: constraint, trigger, partner, artifact, signal) and
*the latitude* (local customization: what each CSM decides for their own customer). The table
below is the index.

| # | Play | Clears | Stage | CSM owns | Partner leads | Artifact produced | Definition of done | Typical |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** | **Prove One Thing** | Value evidence | 0 | Use-case scoping, session, outcome capture | Professional Services (builds it) | Before/after on one existing workflow + one number | Customer states the result in their own words | 4–6 wks |
| **P2** | **Re-Sponsor** | Sponsorship | 1–2 | Sponsor mapping, exec re-engagement | Sales / Renewals | Named exec owner + written charter + ≥3 threads | New sponsor commits to a named outcome and a date | 6–8 wks |
| **P3** | **Kickoff Re-Contract** | Adoption | 1 | User-layer discovery, success criteria | Professional Services | Success criteria written by the *user* lead, not us | The skeptical team's lead publicly commits | 3 wks |
| **P4** | **Quantify & Translate** | Value evidence *(commercial audience)* | 2–3 | Baseline/current, narrative, exec readout | Sales / Value Validation (validates numbers) | Value narrative in the buyer's language + evidence pack | The economic buyer repeats the number back | 3–4 wks |
| **P5** | **Governance Case** | Value evidence *(governance audience)* | 3 | Review-board relationship, sequencing | Security & Risk + Product | AI risk & controls case, reference architecture | Review board approves ≥1 use case | 6–10 wks |
| **P6** | **Governance Partner Insert** | Governance | 2 (fast-moving) | Standards proposal, exec re-entry | Professional Services + Security & Risk | Agent inventory + governance standard + cost/sprawl view | Customer adopts the standard; CS is in the build path | 4–6 wks |
| **P7** | **Spread From the Pocket** | Adoption / Governance | 2 | Internal referral motion, CoE seeding | Professional Services | CoE charter + second team onboarded | A second team in production under a governing body | 8–12 wks |
| **P8** | **Renewal Value Review** | Value evidence | any, at T–2Q | Runs the review | Sales / Renewals | Dated value narrative + expansion hypothesis | Zero surprises at renewal | 2 wks |

### The governance motion is a required play, not an aspiration

Plays **P5, P6, and P7** are one motion viewed from three angles, and the Director owns it across
the book. It's the structural answer to pattern 2 (person-dependence): the durable fix is a
structure, not a better person.

| Account | Governance artifact | Play | What it unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Meridian** | Risk-and-controls case for the review board | P5 | Clears the gate stuck a quarter; releases the blocked expansion |
| **Voltaic** | Agent inventory + governance standard | P6 | The wedge back to relevance before the pricing negotiation |
| **Floor & Board** | Lightweight CoE | P7 | Makes the transformation survive the next champion's departure |

**Current assignment across the book:**

| Account | Constraint | Primary play | Then |
|---|---|---|---|
| Floor & Board | Sponsorship | **P2** Re-Sponsor | **P7** stand up a CoE (structural) |
| Meridian | Value evidence *(gov. audience)* | **P5** Governance Case | P4 |

Six accounts, six different plays, one diagnostic. That's the point — the variation lives in the
play library, not in the CSM's personality.

## 5 · Value realization, and how it becomes money

A value narrative is **an artifact with a date and a status**, not a conversation that happened.
That distinction is the entire commercial mechanism.

```
adoption + commercial signals
        ↓
baseline vs. current on a specific workflow      ← baseline → instrument → quantify → narrate
        ↓
converted into the economic buyer's metric       ← AI drafts this; a human owns it
        ↓
validated by the customer champion
        ↓
repeated back by the economic buyer              ← this is the only real "done"
        ↓
carried into renewal / expansion
```

**Status is tracked, and it gates everything:** `Draft → Reviewed → Customer-validated → Stale`.

- **Renewals are lost in the gap** between "they use it a lot" and "here's what it was worth."
  Floor & Board will renew on relationship or not at all, because nothing has been made legible
  since kickoff.
- **Expansions are won by closing that gap early**, so that procurement argues about scope instead
  of value. Corvus is the case: a significant expansion currently unpurchasable for want of one
  translated artifact.
- **A stale value narrative is a churn signal.** The base flags any account inside two quarters of
  renewal whose narrative is older than a quarter, in the same view and with the same weight as a
  support escalation. It's earlier and more predictive than most churn signals we have — and it's
  why *value narratives ready ≥1 quarter before renewal* is a leading metric I own.

**The honest limit:** AI drafts the narrative; it does not validate it. A number that hasn't been
confirmed by the customer is a hypothesis, and walking a hypothesis into a CFO conversation is
worse than walking in with nothing. The status field exists to make that impossible to do by
accident.

## 6 · Why this survives 15 people of varied depth

| Design choice | The failure it prevents |
|---|---|
| Constraint picks the play, not the CSM | A CSM defaulting to the one play they're personally good at |
| Evidence required for scores of 4–5 | Relationship-led optimism inflating the book |
| Partner explicitly named on every play | A CSM failing alone at a conversation they were never equipped for |
| Skills matrix + deliberate pairing | An account's outcome tracking one CSM's personal strengths |
| Definition of done is customer behavior, not our activity | "We delivered the deck" counting as done |
| Small library, CSM-authored additions | A playbook nobody reads, owned by a director nobody asks |
| Every artifact lives in one base | Fifteen private spreadsheets and no inspection surface |

The system carries the pattern recognition. **The CSM has to own the diagnosis and the narrative.
They never have to be the smartest technical person in the room** — that's what partners and
pairing are for (see [`04-operating-model.md`](04-operating-model.md)).
